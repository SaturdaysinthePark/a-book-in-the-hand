A cockroach, a spiritual unraveling, and your cure for a reading slump
A note

Welcome back, it feels good to be consistent in sharing what I read. I definitely underestimate what it takes to stay on top of reading and reviewing, its so easy to just read and skip sharing.

I read some interesting books this month, but I don't think I'd highly recommend them. Each were quite unique and offbeat perhaps. What I will recommend in general is reading Clarice Lispector. She has quite a unique voice and style. She comes from Brazil and considering I've spent a good amount of time there over the years, I've felt I wanted to read more novels from authors there. If you have suggestions please reach out!

— Sabtain
01 — Featured July review

The Passion According to G.H.

Clarice Lispector
★★★☆☆

A wealthy Rio sculptor's stream of consciousness unravels entirely after she encounters a cockroach in her maid's room, spiraling into something between a breakdown and a spiritual awakening. There's no plot to speak of, just Lispector's rhythmic, erratic prose stripping away everything performative about the 'self'.

02 — From the archive

Death and the Dervish

Meša Selimović
★★★★☆

I stumbled upon this novel in early 2024 and still think about it and recommend it at times. It reads a little Kafka-esque but set in Bosnia during the rule of the Ottoman empire. So if that's your jam, you'll fall right into this. Really enjoyed reading this and loved contemplating the internal struggle the protagonist goes through dealing with politics, bureaucracy, inner and outer faith, and his own idea of morality through detachment.
